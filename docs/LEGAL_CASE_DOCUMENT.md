## PART I — FACTUAL BACKGROUND AND OWNERSHIP HISTORY

### 1.1 Acquisition of Title — First Transaction (1999)

The Petitioner acquired 2 Kanals 3 Marlas in Joint Khata No. 14, Chak No. 72/NP, Tehsil and District Rahim Yar Khan, by virtue of registered Sale Deed No. 2544 dated 11 September 1999. This acquisition is not disputed. The PLRA Director General's own Representation Order No. PLRA/HR/2026/2343 dated 04/11/2026 expressly acknowledges this purchase at paragraph dealing with Petitioner's original land entitlement.

### 1.2 Acquisition of Title — Second Transaction (2002)

The Petitioner subsequently acquired a further 3 Kanals 10 Marlas in the same Khata by virtue of registered Sale Deed No. 262 dated 24 January 2002. The DG PLRA's Representation Order again expressly acknowledges this transaction.

**Total lawful holding as at 24.01.2002: 5 Kanals 13 Marlas**

### 1.3 Revenue Record Discrepancy — Manual Record Error (Pre-2006)

By the time the Jamabandi for 2009–2010 was prepared, the manual revenue record reflected the Petitioner's holding as 7 Kanals 3 Marlas — an excess of 1 Kanal 10 Marlas over his actual lawful holding of 5 Kanals 13 Marlas. The PLRA DG has admitted this discrepancy in his Representation Order, characterising it as a "manual record error."

The Petitioner notes that this excess entry — 1 Kanal 10 Marlas in excess of entitlement — was present in the official record for years before correction and was the foundation of subsequent transactions. The origin, manner, and responsibility for this erroneous entry has never been independently determined.

### 1.4 Sale of 3 Kanals 10 Marlas (2006)

The Petitioner sold 3 Kanals 10 Marlas via Registered Document No. 3942 dated 29 June 2006. After this sale, the Petitioner's lawful remaining holding should have been:
- 5K 13M (total) − 3K 10M (sold) = **2 Kanals 3 Marlas**

This is the Petitioner's own position and is consistent with the acquisition history established under Sale Deeds No. 2544 and No. 262. The PLRA DG's Representation Order acknowledges this sale.

### 1.5 Mutation No. 5424 — Material Date Discrepancy

The departmental position (ARC/RYK letter No. ARC/RYK/26/907 dated 21.07.2026 and the PLRA DG's Representation Order) attributes this 2006 sale to Mutation No. 5424 dated 29.06.2006. However:

- The CLRMIS system record for Mutation No. 5424 reflects a **decision date of 21.08.2014** and an **entry date of 28.02.2015** — approximately nine years after the stated deed date.

- More critically, the computerized registry record for Registry No. 5424 reflects a **transaction dated 23.04.2022** concerning property situated in **Ghausia Colony, Rahim Yar Khan**, showing **Rasheed Ahmad as seller** and **Muhammad Ramzan as purchaser** — persons and property entirely unrelated to the Petitioner or Joint Khata No. 14, Chak 72/NP.

This is not a minor administrative discrepancy. The same number (Mutation/Registry 5424) is being used in the official record to simultaneously (a) justify reduction of the Petitioner's landholding and (b) reference a completely different and unrelated transaction on different property. The question of how and by whom Registry/Mutation No. 5424 came to be associated with the Petitioner's Khata, and on what original lawful authority, requires forensic determination.

### 1.6 Fard Badar No. 199 — Reduction of Record (2023)

On 16 June 2023, Fard Badar No. 199 was entered in CLRMIS under Token No. 41. This entry reduced the Petitioner's recorded holding from the previously inflated 3 Kanals 13 Marlas (i.e., after the purported Mutation 5424 reduction from 7K 3M to 3K 13M) to 2 Kanals 3 Marlas — equivalent to deducting the previously acknowledged excess of 1 Kanal 10 Marlas.

**Anomaly 1 — Fard Badar No. 199 Identity Mismatch:** The computerized registry material identifies Entry/Fard Badar No. 199 as a **General Power of Attorney dated 05.03.2010** between **Abid Muhammad and Muhammad Jamil**. These persons, and that transaction type, bear no relationship to the Petitioner, his land, or Joint Khata No. 14, Chak 72/NP. The same Fard Badar number is being used to reference both a GPA transaction on other land and a correction to the Petitioner's land record. This requires forensic investigation.

**Anomaly 2 — Token No. 41 Reused Across Different Dates:** PLRA's "My Service History" for the Petitioner's NIC records Token No. 41 on **19.06.2023 at 05:17 pm** for two entries simultaneously:
- Fard Badar — Correction-Ownership (RYK)
- Fard Badar — Correction-Person (RYK)

The same Token No. 41 appears again on **26.08.2025 at 01:12 pm** for a further:
- Fard Badar — Correction-Person (RYK)

A PLRA system token is a unique transaction identifier. The re-appearance of Token 41 on a different date, in a different transaction category, two years later, is a system anomaly consistent with either token-number recycling, retroactive record alteration, or manipulation of the electronic audit trail. This is a matter for forensic examination of the CLRMIS database, server logs, and authentication records.

### 1.7 Block of Khata — Decent Commercial Center (2024)

On **23 July 2024**, Khata No. 14 was placed under Block-Ownership status in CLRMIS under Blockage ID 2805898. The stated basis was Order No. MC/RYK/MOP/1991 dated 07.05.2024.

**Anomaly 3 — 77-Day Gap Between Administrative Order and CLRMIS Entry:** The order purportedly authorizing the block is dated 07.05.2024. The CLRMIS block entry is dated 23.07.2024 — a gap of **77 days**. No explanation has been provided for this delay. The original order, the chain of receipt and instruction, and the specific officer who entered the block must be determined.

**Anomaly 4 — CLRMIS Record Reflects Earlier Block Pre-Dating Administrative Order:** The CLRMIS screen captures available to the Petitioner also reflect a **block entry dated 12.07.2023 at 11:30 am** with the reason "Due to malkiat of markeer commitie" (market committee ownership dispute). This is a **separate and earlier** block entry, predating even the MC/RYK/MOP/1991 order of 07.05.2024. The authority for this earlier block, and its relationship to the subsequent Decent Commercial Center block, requires clarification.

**Anomaly 5 — ADLR Column NULL for Both Block and Unblock:** The CLRMIS blockage data for Blockage ID 2805898 records:
- Block (23/07/2024): SCO = kashif.javed.yaseen; **ADLR = NULL**
- Unblock (05/08/2025): SCO = kashif.javed.yaseen; **ADLR = NULL**

Under PLRA procedure, an Additional Director Land Records (ADLR) is required to authorize block and unblock operations. A NULL value in the ADLR field means no senior authorizing officer's credential was recorded for either operation. Both the imposition and the removal of the block were processed without the mandatory supervisory authorization. This is a procedural irregularity of material significance.

**Anomaly 6 — No Service Center Recorded for Block/Unblock Transactions:** The PLRA "My Service History" for the Petitioner confirms:
- Token 11641447 (23/07/2024, Block-Ownership): **Service Center field is blank ("-")**
- Token 14250118 (05/08/2025, Unblock-Ownership): **Service Center field is blank ("-")**

A valid CLRMIS transaction must be associated with a registered Arazi Records Centre. The absence of any Service Center identifier for both the block and the unblock means these transactions cannot be traced to a specific physical ARC, operator workstation, or authorized service-delivery point. This is inconsistent with standard PLRA operating protocol.

**Anomaly 7 — Selective Blocking: Only 3 of 6 Decent Commercial Co-Owners Blocked:**

Joint Khata No. 14 has at least six co-owners with holdings in the same Khata. The CLRMIS blockage data confirms that Blockage ID 2805898 applies **only** to:
1. Muhammad Farooq ولد عبدالستار (CNIC 4420361529293 — 65.2 Marlas)
2. Muhammad Usman ولد Muhammad Zulfiqar — the Petitioner (CNIC 35202-4251249-9 — 33.0 Marlas)
3. Muhammad Zulfiqar ولد عبدالغفور (CNIC 3130320357489 — 188.6 Marlas)

The following co-owners in the **same Khata** were **not** blocked despite the stated Decent Commercial Center basis:
4. Muhammad Umar ولد Muhammad Zulfiqar (CNIC 3130328374897 — 21.8 Marlas) — NOT BLOCKED
5. Muhammad Farooq Fateh ولد فتح محمد (CNIC 3130321402489 — **284.0 Marlas**, largest single holding in Khata) — NOT BLOCKED
6. Ghulam Ghous ولد محمد اسحاق (CNIC 3130324144773 — 35.7 Marlas) — NOT BLOCKED

If the block was placed on account of Decent Commercial Center development concerns, the rationale for blocking only 3 and not all 6 co-owners — particularly not the largest landholder (Muhammad Farooq Fateh with 284 Marlas) — is unexplained and potentially unlawful. Selective application of a block to some co-owners and not others, in the same Khata, requires justification.

## PART III — GROUNDS FOR RELIEF

### Ground A — Breach of Natural Justice / Right to Be Heard (Article 10-A, Constitution)

The Petitioner's lawful title has been materially affected by CLRMIS entries — Mutation 5424, Fard Badar 199, and the Block — without any notice to or hearing of the Petitioner. The DG PLRA's representation hearing on 26.10.2026, while formally conducted, did not examine or determine the underlying documentary and electronic evidence. A formal determination of the cause and responsibility for each discrepancy, with access to the full audit trail, is a minimum constitutional requirement.

### Ground B — Unlawful Block Without Proper Authorization (NULL ADLR)

The imposition of Block-Ownership status on the Petitioner's registered landholding in CLRMIS, without a mandatory ADLR authorization (NULL ADLR field on both block and unblock entries), is procedurally void. The PLRA Regulations and service delivery framework require senior officer authorization for block operations. A block imposed without such authorization has no legal standing and all consequences flowing from it — including the eventual unblock and the out-of-jurisdiction Fard obtained thereafter — are tainted.

### Ground C — Selective Enforcement / Discriminatory Application

The deliberate blocking of only 3 of 6 co-owners in the same Khata, in the context of the same stated Decent Commercial Center basis, while leaving the largest single landholder (284 Marlas) entirely unburdened, prima facie evidences selective and discriminatory application of the block mechanism. This raises a reasonable inference of mala fide intent, warranting judicial inquiry.

### Ground D — Out-of-Jurisdiction Fard (Violation of Territorial Jurisdiction)

The issuance of Fard ID No. 26135745 from ARC Pajian (Raiwind), Lahore Division, for property situated in Rahim Yar Khan, Bahawalpur Division, constitutes a violation of the territorial jurisdiction provisions governing PLRA service delivery. Any document of title issued by an ARC lacking jurisdiction over the subject property cannot constitute a valid basis for subsequent mutation or registration.

### Ground E — Token No. 41 Reuse — Integrity of Electronic Record

The appearance of the same system token number (Token 41) across different transaction categories on different dates (19.06.2023 and 26.08.2025) calls into serious question the integrity of the electronic audit trail in CLRMIS. Under the Electronic Transactions Ordinance 2002 and the Information Technology (Electronic Governance) Act, the authenticity and integrity of electronic records are presumed only where the system has not been shown to be compromised. The Token 41 anomaly, combined with the NULL ADLR and absent Service Center data, provides a prima facie case that the CLRMIS record has been manipulated or is unreliable.

### Ground F — Mutation 5424 and Fard Badar 199 — Identity Contamination

The use of reference numbers (Mutation 5424 and Fard Badar 199) that in the computerized registry correspond to wholly unrelated transactions (a Ghausia Colony property sale and a GPA between unrelated persons respectively) to reduce the Petitioner's recorded holding raises the question of whether these reference numbers were deliberately chosen to obscure the paper trail or were the result of systemic data error. Either explanation requires investigation and rectification.

### Ground G — Contempt of Court

The LHC order dated 09.09.2026 in WP No. 50498/2026 directed a "speak order" within 30 days of receipt of certified copy. The DG PLRA's Representation Order dated 04/11/2026, while formally issued, does not comply with the spirit or substance of the court's direction in that it:

(a) Fails to address the specific system-level anomalies raised by the Petitioner;
(b) Fails to examine or produce the underlying documentary and electronic evidence;
(c) Treats the present displayed arithmetic as determinative without establishing its lawful basis;
(d) Reaches a conclusion adverse to the Petitioner without independent forensic examination of the electronic records.

Where a court orders an authority to "speak" to specific issues and the authority's response systematically avoids those issues while reaching a predetermined conclusion, this is non-compliance in substance and falls within the scope of Contempt Petition Crl. Org. No. 62314/2026.

### Ground H — Preservation Order — Urgency

The Decent Commercial Center project (Building Plan No. MC-RYK-CBG-290624-000063 dated 29.06.2024; MEPCO Demand Notice No. 121455/CE(P&E)/DD dated 23.12.2025) is an active commercial development by Al-Ittihad Developers. Any physical development or further third-party transactions on the basis of the disputed record will cause irreversible prejudice to the Petitioner and all co-owners whose interests are adversely affected. An immediate preservation order on the land record and the CLRMIS electronic records (including server logs, database backups, authentication records, and all data relating to Token 41, Blockage ID 2805898, Mutation 5424, and Fard Badar 199) is urgently required.
